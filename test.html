How Retrieval-Augmented Generation Is Changing the Way We Build FAQ Systems

Vive Team September 14, 2026 8 min read
📚

Traditional FAQ pages are static. They sit in a spreadsheet, get copy-pasted into a CMS, and are promptly forgotten until a support ticket points out they are six months out of date. For small businesses handling hundreds of similar questions a day, this approach simply does not scale.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation — RAG for short — takes a fundamentally different approach. Instead of hand-crafting answers, you feed the system your existing documents (PDFs, Word files, website content) and let the AI synthesise precise, cited answers on demand. The result feels like a knowledgeable team member, not a search bar.

What makes RAG different from a plain chatbot?

A plain LLM (like GPT-4 or Gemini) answers from its training data — which was frozen at a certain date and contains no knowledge of your specific business. Ask it your return policy and it will hallucinate something plausible. RAG solves this by retrieving relevant chunks from your documents first, then asking the LLM to answer using only that retrieved context. The LLM becomes a reasoning layer on top of your own knowledge base, not a replacement for it.

"RAG is not magic — it is engineering. Good document structure, sensible chunk sizes, and a well-tuned retrieval threshold matter more than the choice of LLM model."

Building the pipeline: the three key stages

Every RAG system, no matter how sophisticated, boils down to three stages: ingest, retrieve, and generate. During ingest, your documents are split into overlapping chunks, converted to vector embeddings (numerical representations of meaning), and stored in a vector database such as ChromaDB or Pinecone.

During retrieval, the user query is also converted to an embedding and the database returns the N most semantically similar chunks — not keyword matches, but chunks whose meaning is closest to the question. Finally, during generation, those chunks are injected into a prompt and the LLM generates a grounded, source-cited answer.

💡 Implementation note For the Vive FAQ chatbot we use ChromaDB for local vector storage, OpenAI embeddings (text-embedding-3-small), and GPT-4o-mini for generation. Swap any layer independently — the pipeline is modular by design.

Embedding the widget: the final mile

Once the backend pipeline is running, client-facing delivery is handled by a single JavaScript file — widget.js — that injects a floating chat button onto any existing webpage. No framework dependencies, no build step, no npm required. The client pastes one line and gets a fully functional AI FAQ assistant.
